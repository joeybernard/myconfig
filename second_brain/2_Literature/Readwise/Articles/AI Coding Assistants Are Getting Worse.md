## Metadata
- Author: [[Jamie Twiss]]
- Full Title: AI Coding Assistants Are Getting Worse
- Category: #articles
- Summary: AI coding assistants have stopped improving and now often produce code that looks correct but is wrong. Newer models try to avoid errors by making up data or skipping checks, causing hidden bugs. To fix this, developers need better training data and expert review instead of relying on user acceptance alone.

## Highlights
- After two years of steady improvements, over the course of 2025, most of the core models reached a quality plateau, and more recently, seem to be in decline. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kf1csjhd2hpje4k4n7eah0e6))
- Until recently, the most common problem with AI coding assistants was poor syntax, followed closely by flawed logic. AI-created code would often fail with a syntax error or snarl itself up in faulty structure. This could be frustrating: the solution usually involved manually reviewing the code in detail and finding the mistake. But it was ultimately tractable. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kf1e2dnsg0h00yx0gkapg9ah))
- However, recently released LLMs, such as [GPT-5](https://spectrum.ieee.org/gpt-5-trough-of-disillusionment), have a much more insidious method of failure. They often generate code that fails to perform as intended, but which on the surface seems to run successfully, avoiding syntax errors or obvious crashes. It does this by removing safety checks, or by creating fake output that matches the desired format, or through a variety of other techniques to avoid crashing during execution. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kf1e2wg5y5m62vyjft1r2000))
- As any developer will tell you, this kind of silent failure is far, far worse than a crash. ([View Highlight](https://read.readwise.io/read/01kf1e36nmwsz3prvv6z0z3sgm))
