### 1. Conway’s Law #2

[Conway’s Law #2](http://www.sanjeev.net/murphys-laws/conways-law-2.html) states, “In every organization, there will always be one person who knows what is going on. This person must be fired.”

This seems especially true in government.

### 2. Etorre’s Observaton

[Ettore’s Observation](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Ettore%27s_observation) simply says that, “The other line always moves faster.”

One only has to have stood in line for practically anything to know this to be absolutely true.

### 3. Flat Surface Law

[The Flat Surface Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_flat_surfaces) states, “If given enough time, any flat or horizontal surface will eventually accumulate clutter.”

To verify the truth value of this law, simply take a look around your house or workplace. It is absolutely a universal truth.

### 4. Finagle’s Law

[Finagle’s Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finagle%27s_law) is really is a derivation of Murphy’s Law but has to do with timing. It tells us that “anything that can go wrong, will go wrong — at the worst possible moment.”

For some reason, there are a handful of completely different truisms that are called Finagle’s Law. I simply chose one.

### 5. Finster’s Law

[Finster’s Law](https://ejsol.dse.nl/DSE/Murphys_laws.html#:~:text=FINSTER'S%20RULE%3A,CLOSED%20MOUTH%20GATHERS%20NO%20FEET.) succinctly points out that “a closed mouth gather no feet.”

Of course, Mark Twain pointed this out when he famously said, “It is better to keep your mouth closed and let people think you are a fool than to open it and remove all doubt.”

### 6. Hanlon’s Razor

==[Hanlon’s Razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanlon%27s_razor)== ==states that “one should never attribute to malice that which is adequately explained by stupidity.”==

This razor is credited to Robert J. Hanlon who submitted for publishing in Arthur Bloch’s book, _Murphy’s Law Book Two: More Reasons Why Things Go Wrong!_

### ==7. Hofstadter’s Law==

[Hofstadter’s law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofstadter%27s_law) states that “it always takes longer than you expect, even when you take into account Hofstadter’s law.”

This adage was first stated explicitly by Douglas Hofstadter in 1979 in his book _Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid._

### 8. Jenning’s Law

[Jenning’s Law](https://www.cowles.ca/Murphy.html#:~:text=Jennings%20Corollary%20to%20the%20Law,the%20value%20of%20the%20carpet.) is actually a corollary to Murphy’s Law. It states that “the chance of the bread falling with the buttered side down is directly proportional to the cost of the carpet.”

One would think that the numbers would work out to be 50% of the time. But based on empirical data from my life, the bread certainly lands butter side down much higher than 50%.

### 9. Maier’s Law

[Maier’s Law](https://gwern.net/doc/statistics/bias/publication/1960-maier.pdf) states that “if the facts do not conform to the theory, they must be disposed of.”

Unfortunately, it seems that this law is currently being followed to the letter by U.S. politicians.

### 10. Occam’s Razor

[Occam’s Razor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occam%27s_razor) says that ==“the simplest explanation for a phenomenon is usually the most likely to be true.”==

If you walk into the room and go to turn the light switch on and nothing happens, it could be that rats have eaten through your electrical wires, your check bounced to the electric company, or the world is involved in global nuclear war.

Or, the light bulb could be burnt out. Occam’s razor would recommend checking that first.
