## Highlights
- It was 1372 when king Satto, the emperor of Ryukyu Kingdom (territory of the Ryukyu archipelago whose largest island is Okinawa) and the Chinese king of Ming dynasty established the official diplomatic relations. That marked the beginning of the era of comprehensive trade between two nations, during which Okinawans adopted many aspects of Chinese culture, including Chinese fighting system Kenpo Ch’uanfa). That was the base for the later development of karate on the small island of Okinawa. To-te (Chinese fighting techniques) were studied and gradually merged with indigenous Te techniques (hand techniques, including leg kicks). Te, the word that marks the original Okinawa fighting system is pronounced «tiy». ([Location 606](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0BBZ8TZ3T&location=606))
- Until the early 20th century, during the several years after Okinawa became Japanese administrative area, karate was included into the curriculum of Physical Education in Okinawan public schools. It was transferred from there to the Japanese mainland, where it became well-known and respected. ([Location 618](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0BBZ8TZ3T&location=618))
- Consequently, karate training, although it retained the ability to create competent fighters, was also developed into the means of entire personal development. A modern karate-do is, technically speaking, a martial art of self-defense. In spiritual sense, this is a martial path of self-improvement. These two principles should not be separated, but considered as united, in order to develop the practitioner of karate-do into a healthy and coordinated personality, physically, as well as mentally. ([Location 628](https://readwise.io/to_kindle?action=open&asin=B0BBZ8TZ3T&location=628))
