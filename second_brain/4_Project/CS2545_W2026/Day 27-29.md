Section 5.2 - Components of Time Series Analysis

4 Components of a time series
- trend - the long-term direction
- cyclic component - large variations over longer time periods
- seasonal components - variations that happen over a fixed time scale
- noise/random error - random, unpredictable variation

Often, the trend and cyclic components are combined into the trend-cycle component.

These components can be combined as:
- additive decomposition - the time series is made up of the components added together
- multiplicative decomposition - the components are multiplied together
- hybrid decomposition - some combination of the above

The trend-cycle component, or the trend curve, pulls out this component. There is no single perfect way to decide the trendline.

The noise component is given by the residuals from the measured values and the model values. It should be white noise:
- constant zero mean
- constant variance
- uncorrelated
## Example of multiplicative decomposition
Key Example: Monthly Airline Passenger Data

The classic example of a multiplicative time series is the international airline passenger dataset (or similar monthly retail sales data). ![GitHub](https://encrypted-tbn1.gstatic.com/faviconV2?url=https://sthalles.github.io&client=AIM&size=128&type=FAVICON&fallback_opts=TYPE,SIZE,URL)GitHub +1

- **The Trend:** The number of passengers increases over the years (upward trend).
- **The Seasonality:** Every year, there is a peak in travel during summer months (June/July) and a drop in winter.
- **The Multiplicative Behavior:** As the overall trend increases (more total people flying), the absolute difference between the summer peak and winter low **also grows larger**.
    - _Example:_ If in 1950, summer passengers were 10,000 more than winter, by 1960, the summer peak might be 50,000 more than winter. The _proportion_ of increase is consistent, not the _absolute amount_. 


Other Examples

- **Web Traffic:** A website that is growing in popularity (trend) may see daily spikes in traffic on weekdays that grow larger in magnitude as the total user base increases.
- **Retail Sales:** A store where holiday sales (e.g., December) are consistently 50% higher than the monthly average, regardless of whether the business is growing or shrinking overall.
- **Financial Data:** A company's revenue that shows quarterly seasonal surges that are relative to the overall upward growth of the business.
