Section 2.2

- survey design is hard
- open-ended questions - allows for in-depth responses, unexpected insights
- closed-ended questions - predefined responses, better for quantitative data
- important to try and avoid bias in questions
	- do you prefer our brand over cheaper alternatives
	- what factors do you consider when choosing a brand
- sampling - how to select participants in order to fairly represent your population
	- simple random sampling
	- stratified sampling - split the group into sub-groups based on some factors and take a random sampling from each sub-group
	- cluster sampling - cluster into sub-groups based on natural categories (e.g. communities), select clusters at random and survey all members of the cluster
	- convenience sampling - choose members based on the ease of engaging them - classic person with a clipboard
	- systematic sampling - starting at a random point in the data set, take every n-th item - OK if truly random, and structure in the data could lead to bias
	- purposive sampling - choosing members based on some characteristic
	- snowball sampling - used when accessing population may be difficult, relies on assumption that people with similar characteristics or experiences tend to associate with each other
	- quota sampling - use pre-defined quotas to make sure that you have n samples from each category are included - non-probability technique
	- volunteer sampling - don't pick members at all, allow for people to take part - can include bias
- sampling error - difference between results based on a sample compared to actual results for the population
- sampling bias - 
- measurement error - problems with the data collection
	- random errors
	- systematic errors
